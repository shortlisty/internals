# Pitch

> [!NOTE]
> Personal Venue Catalog segment reference. Not the primary direction — see [Digital Sales Room](../../../digital-sales-room-for-events/README.md).

> **Audience:** Founders, team.
> **Purpose:** Narrative arc for an intro call or live demo. One flow that can be shortened or extended depending on the conversation.

---

## When to use this

This is not a script. It is a sequence of beats with the goal of each beat and the transition into the next. Adapt it to the conversation — if the prospect volunteers the problem unprompted, skip to the demo. If they push back on AI accuracy early, go straight to the confidence score story.

Target length: 20–30 minutes for a full run. A cold intro call should hit beats 1–3 only, then book a demo.

---

## The flow

### Beat 1 — Set the scene (2 min)

**Goal:** make the prospect feel seen before showing anything.

Ask one question: "When a client calls with a specific brief, how do you find the right venue right now?"

Let them answer. Do not interrupt. The answer will be some version of: email search, shared drive, asking a colleague, memory. That is the problem.

Transition: "That's exactly what we built VenueMi to fix."

---

### Beat 2 — The problem in one sentence (1 min)

**Goal:** name the pain precisely so the prospect nods.

> "The knowledge your team has built up over years — every venue you've visited, every deck you've received, every detail you've verified — it's all there. It's just buried in files. VenueMi gives it a brain. It's venue portfolio management, built for planners."

Do not over-explain. Two sentences, then move.

---

### Beat 3 — The aha moment demo (5–10 min)

**Goal:** show, don't tell. The product earns trust in 30 seconds if the demo is right.

Demo sequence:

1. Start with an empty venue — just a name and address.
2. Upload a real venue PDF deck. Use a realistic one, not a clean test file.
3. Watch the extraction run. Show the confidence scores appearing per field.
4. Show the consolidated metadata card: capacity in every configuration, catering policy, AV specs, contacts.
5. Run a natural-language search: "venues with kosher catering for 150, no curfew." Show it return the venue.
6. Show the source citation — click a field, it opens the exact page of the PDF it came from.

Pause after step 4 and ask: "Is that the kind of data you'd want on every venue in your library?"

---

### Beat 4 — The team angle (3 min)

**Goal:** shift from individual value to organisational value. This is where the buying trigger lives.

> "Right now, your venue portfolio exists in one person's head or one person's inbox. When they're on holiday, or when they leave, it's gone. VenueMi makes the portfolio shared and permanent — a single library every planner on your team can search, that grows with every new document you add. Every new hire starts with the full portfolio on day one."

If the prospect is an agency owner: lean on the knowledge-retention and portfolio-as-asset angle — they've invested years building those relationships and that intelligence should outlast any individual.
If the prospect is a head of events at a corporate: lean on the "stop repeating the same research every event cycle" angle — the portfolio compounds, each event makes the next one cheaper to research.

---

### Beat 5 — Pricing and next step (3 min)

**Goal:** remove friction from the decision.

> "Free up to ten venues — no credit card. Most people understand what the product does the moment they see a PDF turn into a profile. Pro is $49 a month for your whole team, up to 500 venues."

Offer concierge onboarding: "We can import your first 20 venues for you — just send us the files. That gets you past the empty-library problem on day one."

Close with a specific next step: "Want to try it with one of your actual venue decks right now?"

---

## Objections in this call

If an objection comes up during the pitch, see [objections.md](objections.md) for handling guidance. Do not break the demo flow to debate — acknowledge, park, continue, then address at the end.

---

## What not to do

- Do not lead with the technology (Spring AI, pgvector, ETL pipeline). Nobody buying a planner tool cares.
- Do not demo a clean, simple PDF. Use a messy real-world one. The harder the document, the more impressive the result.
- Do not pitch the marketplace or Phase 3 features. They are not available and the prospect cannot buy them.
- Do not quote implementation timelines or engineering roadmap dates.
