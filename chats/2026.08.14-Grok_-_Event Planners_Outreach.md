**LinkedIn is the strongest primary channel**, followed by industry associations/directories, online marketplaces, social platforms, and in-person events. Here’s a practical playbook to find and connect with event agencies, corporate event leads (in-house or agency), and solo planners who handle weddings, corporate events, conferences, and venues.

### 1. LinkedIn (Highest ROI for B2B/Corporate + Many Solo Planners)

Use advanced search or **Sales Navigator** (highly recommended for volume and filters):

**Target titles / keywords:**

- Event Planner / Senior Event Planner / Lead Event Planner
- Corporate Event Manager / Director of Corporate Events / Head of Events
- Wedding Planner / Luxury Wedding Planner / Destination Wedding Planner
- Conference Manager / Meeting Planner / Convention Manager
- Venue Manager / Event Venue Manager / Director of Events (at venues/hotels)
- Experiential Marketing Manager / Special Events Manager
- DMC (Destination Management Company) contacts
- Freelancer / Independent Event Planner / Solo Planner

**Filters that work well:**

- Location (city/region or “United States,” “Europe,” etc.)
- Industry: Events Services, Hospitality, Marketing & Advertising, Nonprofit, Corporate
- Company size (for agencies vs. in-house)
- Keywords in profile: “wedding,” “corporate events,” “conferences,” “venue management,” “MICE,” “full-service planning”

**Tactics:**

- Search Boolean-style: `("corporate event" OR "wedding planner" OR "conference planner") AND ("venue" OR "agency" OR "independent")`
- Join active LinkedIn groups: Event Planning & Event Management, BizBash groups, Event Pros, Meeting Professionals International-related groups, local event planner groups.
- Engage first (comment on posts, share relevant content) then send personalized connection requests referencing their recent events or niche.
- Use hashtags: #EventPlanner, #CorporateEvents, #WeddingPlanner, #MeetingProfs, #EventIndustry, #VenueManager.

Many solo planners and smaller agencies are highly active here and open to conversations about tools, venues, partnerships, or services.

### 2. Industry Associations & Membership Directories

These put you directly in front of decision-makers:

| Association                                               | Focus                           | Why Useful                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **MPI** (Meeting Professionals International)             | Corporate meetings, conferences | Large membership of planners & suppliers    |
| **PCMA** (Professional Convention Management Association) | Conventions & large events      | High-level corporate & association planners |
| **IAVM** (International Association of Venue Managers)    | Venue managers                  | Direct access to people who manage venues   |
| **WIPA** (Wedding Industry Professionals Association)     | Wedding planners & vendors      | Chapters worldwide, networking events       |
| **NACE** (National Association for Catering and Events)   | Catering + events               | Strong planner community                    |
| **IAEE**                                                  | Exhibitions & trade shows       | Conference/exhibition organizers            |
| **IACC**                                                  | Conference centers              | Venue-focused professionals                 |
| Regional ones (AEO/AEV in UK, AEVEA in Spain, etc.)       | Local markets                   | Often have public or member directories     |

Many publish member directories, host local chapter events, or run conferences where you can network. Join as a supplier/partner if possible, or attend open events.

### 3. Online Directories & Marketplaces

- **Wedding-focused**: The Knot, Zola, WeddingWire, PartySlate, Wezoree (premium/destination), local equivalents (Hitched in UK, etc.).
- **Broader events**: EventPlanning.com, FindEventProfs.com / Event-Directory.com, Cvent Vendor Marketplace, Hire Space (venues + planners), Bark.com, GreatEvent.
- **Venue-related**: Search “venue managers association” or platforms like WEVA (Wedding/Event Venue Association).
- Google: “event agencies [city]”, “corporate event planners [city]”, “wedding planners [city] directory”.

These often list agencies, solo planners, and sometimes venue contacts with websites/emails for outreach.

### 4. Social Media & Communities (Especially for Solo & Wedding Planners)

- **Instagram**: Search hashtags #WeddingPlanner, #CorporateEventPlanner, #EventPlannerLife, #VenueManager + location. Many solo planners and small agencies post portfolios and are responsive to DMs.
- **Facebook Groups**: Local “Wedding Planners [City]”, “Event Professionals Network”, venue owner groups.
- **X (Twitter)**: Follow and search for event industry accounts; use advanced search for recent discussions.
- Reddit: r/eventplanning, r/weddingplanning (more consumer-facing but planners sometimes post), industry-specific subs.

### 5. Events, Trade Shows & Networking

Attend or exhibit at:

- Event Planner Expo / similar regional expos
- MPI, PCMA, IAEE, WIPA conferences and chapter meetups
- Bridal shows / wedding industry events
- Venue open days or supplier networking nights
- Hosted-buyer or matchmaking programs at larger shows

In-person is still one of the fastest ways to build real relationships with venue managers and agency leads.

### 6. Practical Outreach Tips

- **Personalize heavily** — reference a specific event they ran, a venue they manage, or content they shared.
- Lead with value (industry insight, potential partnership idea, free resource) rather than a hard pitch.
- For corporate leads inside companies: Start with receptionist/HR or search for “Director of Special Events / Meetings” titles; many evolved into the role without the exact title.
- Track everything in a simple CRM or spreadsheet (name, company, source, last contact).
- Combine channels: Find someone on LinkedIn → check their agency website or Instagram → reference both in outreach.

### Quick Start Plan

1. Set up LinkedIn Sales Navigator (or free advanced search) and build 2–3 saved searches for your target niches/locations.
2. Join 3–5 relevant associations or groups and attend one virtual/local event this month.
3. Browse 2–3 directories (The Knot + EventPlanning.com + a local one) and export/save 20–30 promising profiles.
4. Start light engagement + personalized outreach.

This approach consistently surfaces agencies, in-house corporate leads, and independent planners who actively manage or book venues for weddings, corporate events, and conferences. Results improve dramatically once you narrow by geography or specific event type (luxury weddings vs. large conferences, etc.). **Yes.** Event agencies, corporate event leads, and solo planners (especially those handling weddings, corporate events, conferences, and venues) rely heavily on a mix of specialized and general tools. Many have strong mobile apps or mobile-optimized access because planners work on-site, on the go, and need real-time updates.

Here’s a clear breakdown of the most commonly used and targeted tools (based on 2025–2026 industry usage patterns):

### 1. All-in-One Business / CRM Hubs (Most Popular for Solo Planners & Small Agencies)

These handle leads, proposals, contracts, invoices, payments, client portals, and basic project tracking. Highly mobile-friendly.

| Tool                                 | Best For                             | Key Strengths                                                           | Mobile Notes          |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| **HoneyBook**                        | Wedding & boutique event planners    | Polished client experience, proposals, contracts, payments, automations | Excellent mobile app  |
| **Dubsado**                          | Solo/automation-heavy planners       | Deeply customizable workflows, forms, branding                          | Strong mobile access  |
| **Aisle Planner**                    | Wedding planners specifically        | Timelines, design boards, vendor collaboration, seating                 | Mobile-friendly       |
| **Planning Pod**                     | Mixed corporate + venue work         | Budgets, floor plans, BEOs, multi-event calendars, venue booking        | Good mobile support   |
| **Agiled** / **17hats** / **Bonsai** | Budget-conscious solos & small teams | CRM + invoicing + contracts + projects in one                           | Mobile apps available |

HoneyBook and Aisle Planner dominate among wedding-focused independents. Planning Pod is stronger when venues or corporate work are involved.

### 2. Project & Task Management (Used Across All Segments)

- **Asana** — Favorite for corporate/conference teams and multi-stakeholder projects (strong timelines & collaboration).
- **Monday.com** — Visual boards popular with mid-size agencies running concurrent events.
- **ClickUp** / **Trello** / **Notion** — Flexible (and often free or low-cost) options many solos and smaller teams prefer.
- **Timeline Genius** — Specialized for creating professional, vendor-filtered run-of-show timelines (very common with wedding planners).

These almost all have solid mobile apps for checking tasks on-site.

### 3. Floor Plans, Seating & Venue Design

- **Social Tables** (free tier available) — Industry standard for seating charts and floor plans.
- Prismm (formerly Allseated), Merri (3D design), and various seating chart tools.
- Venue-specific software often includes these features.

Mobile access is useful for on-site adjustments.

### 4. Enterprise / Large Conference Tools (Corporate Event Leads & Agencies)

- **Cvent** — Dominant for large corporate programs, venue sourcing, registration, and on-site management.
- **Bizzabo**, **Whova**, **RainFocus**, **Eventbrite** (more for ticketing/public events).
- These have dedicated mobile apps for attendees _and_ organizers (check-in, agenda, networking, real-time updates).

### 5. Venue Management Specific Tools

Venue managers and planners who book or operate spaces frequently use:

- **Event Temple**, **Tripleseat**, **VenueOps**, **Planning Pod**, **Mazévo**.
- These focus on bookings, availability calendars, proposals, BEOs (Banquet Event Orders), and operations. Most have mobile or tablet-friendly interfaces for floor staff and sales teams.

### 6. Everyday Supporting Tools Nearly Everyone Uses

- **Canva** — Design for invitations, signage, mood boards, social posts (ubiquitous).
- **WhatsApp / iMessage / Slack** — Real-time vendor and team communication (especially day-of).
- Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 for shared docs and calendars.
- Payment processors (Stripe is common under the hood of many CRM tools).

### Key Patterns by Persona

- **Solo wedding planners**: HoneyBook or Aisle Planner + Timeline Genius + Social Tables + Canva + WhatsApp.
- **Corporate event leads / agencies**: Cvent or Bizzabo + Asana/Monday + Social Tables + internal tools.
- **Venue managers**: Specialized venue software (Event Temple, Tripleseat, etc.) + calendar tools.
- **Mixed / independent agencies**: Often a “hub” (HoneyBook/Dubsado/Planning Pod) + project tool + design tools.

Most of these platforms prioritize **mobile apps or responsive mobile web** because planners constantly work from venues, vendor meetings, or while traveling. Day-of coordination especially depends on mobile access for checklists, timelines, guest lists, and real-time updates.
